# Image Foundations — Deep Research Report

**Prepared:** July 26, 2026
**Purpose:** Research foundation for a future "Image Foundations" member training series (analogous to Copywriting Foundations) on selecting and evaluating images for native-ad affiliate campaigns (Taboola/Realize, Outbrain/Teads, MGID, Revcontent) promoting Media Mavens and ClickBank offers.
**Method:** 5 parallel focus-area research passes + 2 gap-fill passes + 1 example harvest, ~70 distinct sources consulted, tier-rated (Tier 1 = academic/government/official primary docs; Tier 2 = major publication/law-firm/industry research org; Tier 3 = vendor blog/promotional). Raw research transcripts: `.agents/outputs/image-foundations/raw-*.md`. Example creatives: [`examples/INDEX.md`](examples/INDEX.md).
**Scope exclusions (deliberate):** A/B-testing methodology and statistics; offer-specific "winning image" lists.

---

## Executive summary

1. **Image jobs are stage-dependent, and the framework is validated.** The native ad thumbnail's job is qualified attention and a click promise; the advertorial hero's job is congruence (confirming the promise and sustaining "information scent"); the jump-page/pre-VSL image's job is credibility and purchase-intention support. This maps onto Tier-1 literature: information-foraging/scent theory, processing-fluency congruence research, post-click-quality penalties for pure curiosity plays, and the Ohanian source-credibility lineage. No single study tests all three stages together — the framework is a well-grounded synthesis, not a single citation (§4).
2. **Faces and gaze are the most replicated attention levers.** Faces capture first fixation; the face's *gaze direction* then transfers attention. Direct gaze builds connection/trust; gaze aimed at the product/text transfers attention and can raise purchase intention. A face with no directional cue risks capturing attention without transferring it (§1).
3. **Curiosity-gap imagery is now directly supported, with a sharp boundary.** Visual concealment/partial reveal in the image itself experimentally raises curiosity and preference — but *only when the underlying inference is positive*, and networks/platforms penalize visual bait whose landing page doesn't pay it off (§1.4, §4.2).
4. **Color folklore is mostly wrong; saturation and contrast-vs-feed are what replicate.** "Red = urgency, blue = trust" fails as a universal. What replicates: saturation → perceived potency/efficacy (with a "gentle product" boundary), color-brand congruence, and salience *relative to the surrounding feed* rather than any absolute hue (§2).
5. **"UGC beats polished" is conditionally true, not a law.** Tier-1 meta-analytic support exists for authenticity/UGC advantages, but polished/crafted imagery wins for luxury, high-price, and credibility-stage contexts. Teach the conditional version (§3.3).
6. **Compliance is convergent across networks.** Taboola, Outbrain, MGID, and Revcontent independently ban: before/after photos, sexualized body-part emphasis, fake UI elements, celebrity/politician imagery without documented permission, shock/gore, and misleading crops. FTC doctrine treats the *image itself* as capable of making a claim that requires substantiation. One consolidated no-no list is in §5.
7. **34 harvested example creatives** (Taboola ebooks, Joinative lookbook, MGID advertorial screenshots) are indexed in `examples/INDEX.md` with per-item principle mapping — reference-only pending human rights review.

## 1. Visual attention & image psychology

### 1.1 Faces and gaze cueing (replication status: robust)

- **Core effect:** Faces reliably capture attention and combat banner blindness; the face's gaze direction then steers attention. Palcu, Sudkamp & Florack (Frontiers in Psychology, 2017 — the banner gaze-cuing study) found gaze-toward-product increased both looking at the product and purchase intention ([link](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00881/full), Tier 1).
- **Independent replications:** Hutton & Nolte (Applied Cognitive Psychology, 2011 — print ads, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.1763), Tier 1); D'Ambrogio et al. (Psychology & Marketing, 2023 — eye-tracking + pupillometry + drift-diffusion, N=77; gaze cueing and celebrity status both bias choice, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.21772), Tier 1); Adil, Lacoste-Badie & Droulers (Journal of Advertising Research, 2018 — face presence + gaze direction in print, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2501/jar-2018-004), Tier 1); Xiao & Ding (Marketing Science, 2014 — facial features measurably affect brand outcomes, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2013.0837), Tier 1).
- **The direct-vs-averted tradeoff:** To & Patrick, "How the Eyes Connect to the Heart" (Journal of Consumer Research, 2021, 48(1):123–146, [DOI 10.1093/jcr/ucaa063](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucaa063), Tier 1) show *averted* gaze can increase effectiveness via immersion/narrative transportation. Practical rule: **direct gaze = connection/trust; gaze aimed at the product or headline = attention transfer.** A face with neither (looking off-frame at nothing relevant) captures attention without transferring it.
- **Caution on "universal facial expressions":** the popular Ekman claim is contested. Gendron, Crivelli & Barrett (Current Directions in Psychological Science, 2018, [PDF](https://affective-science.org/pubs/2018/gendron-et-al-universality-cdps.pdf), Tier 1) document substantial cross-cultural diversity in reading facial expressions. Emotional-face imagery works, but don't teach "everyone reads this face the same way" as fact.

### 1.2 Picture superiority & picture-word consistency

- Houston, Childers & Heckler (Journal of Marketing Research, 1987, [link](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/002224378702400403), Tier 1): picture-word *consistency* improves elaborative processing and memory. Supporting lineage: Childers & Houston 1986 (Psychology & Marketing); Heckler & Childers 1992 (JCR, expectancy/relevancy framework, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1086/209275)).
- Implication: the image should visualize the same idea as the headline — mismatch costs encoding and recall, and (per §4) costs post-click quality.

### 1.3 Visual complexity: inverted-U, small-size penalty

- JAMS 2025 ("The dynamic effects of visual complexity and scene cuts on viewer attention," [link](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-025-01137-x), Tier 1) and IJRM 2025 (visual complexity × ad effectiveness, Tier 1) support a non-monotonic complexity-attention relationship: too plain is ignorable, too busy is aversive. The exact functional form (strict inverted-U vs threshold) remains open — teach "moderate, single-subject complexity," not a precise curve.
- At thumbnail render sizes, complexity tolerance shrinks further; platform doctrine ("simplicity over fine, small details" — Taboola) is consistent with the eye-tracking literature (MDPI Applied Sciences 2023 banner complexity study, Tier 1/2).

### 1.4 Curiosity gap in images (upgraded from inference to direct evidence)

- **Primary theory:** Loewenstein, "The Psychology of Curiosity" (Psychological Bulletin, 1994, [PDF](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf), Tier 1) — replaces the previously mis-cited Cambridge learning-handbook source.
- **Direct visual evidence:** "Leaving Something for the Imagination: The Effect of Visual Concealment on Preferences" (Journal of Marketing, 2020, six experiments, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022242919899393), Tier 1): partially concealing the product in the *image* arouses curiosity and inflates preference — **only when initial inferences are positive** ("products that benefit most have the least to hide"). Grounded further by Collins & Litman's perceptual-curiosity construct (Personality & Individual Differences, 2003, Tier 1) and occlusion-as-attention-cue work (Liao, SAGE Open, 2023, Tier 2).
- **The boundary:** visual bait that the landing page doesn't pay off is (a) penalized by platforms' post-click quality models (Yahoo KDD 2015; Zhou et al., WWW 2016 native-ads pre-click quality) and (b) definitional visual clickbait in the detection literature (CHECKER, ECML-PKDD 2021). Curiosity must *pre-qualify*, not merely maximize clicks.
- **Honest gap:** no study isolates a curiosity-gap image from its headline in a live native feed; the concealment experiments are lab/preference studies. Marked as a limitation (§7).

## 2. Color: science vs folklore

### What replicates (Tier 1)

- **Saturation → perceived potency/efficacy.** Labrecque et al., "Color Me Effective" (Journal of Marketing, 2024: 7 experiments n=2,745 + scraping study + field experiment, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222429241296392)): higher saturation → inferred efficacy, generalizing to **packaging and ad background color**. Boundary: reverses when the buyer wants a "gentle" product (relevant to some health/beauty angles).
- **Saturation → perceived size/heft; "attention-grabbing" is a saturation effect, not a hue effect.** Hagtvedt & Brasel (JCR, 2017, [link](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/44/2/396/3061448)).
- **Color-in-context theory:** color meanings are learned and context-dependent (Elliot & Maier, Annual Review of Psychology, 2014; Elliot, Frontiers in Psychology, 2015). There is **no universal color-attribute map**.
- **Congruence beats hue:** Labrecque & Milne, "Exciting Red and Competent Blue" (JAMS, 2012, 208 brands): consistency between color and brand personality predicts preference better than any absolute color choice.

### Myths to bust explicitly

- **"Red = urgency" is bidirectional:** Bagchi & Cheema (JCR, 2013): red increases competitive-auction bids but *decreases* negotiation offers. Context flips the sign.
- **"Blue = trust" fails as a universal** — congruence is the driver (JAMS 2012 above).
- **"Color drives 62–90% of judgment"** traces to Singh 2006 (Management Decision) describing *initial visual judgment components*, not a color-to-sales correlation; widely misquoted in vendor blogs.

### Contrast-vs-feed beats hue (practitioner-scale, honest tiering)

- Matt Heisig / HitFactor, 1.53M-video YouTube thumbnail analysis (Tier 3, LinkedIn): publicly reversed "crank the saturation" advice — salience is **relative to the surrounding feed**, not absolute. Directionally corroborated by banner eye-tracking (MDPI Future Internet 2021) and product-background color-combination effects (Huang, Psychology & Marketing, 2020, Tier 1).
- Native-feed implication: most native placements sit in white editorial pages; Taboola's own guidance ("avoid black-and-white — blends into the feed") is the platform version of the same principle. No direct academic study of ad-image background contrast *in native feeds specifically* was found (limitation, §7).

## 3. Composition & framing at thumbnail scale

### 3.1 Platform doctrine (official specs, Tier 3-official but authoritative for their own platforms)

| Rule | Taboola/Realize | Outbrain/Teads |
|---|---|---|
| Canonical size | 1200×674 (16:9), JPEG, ≤5 MB; thumbnail min 600×400 | 1200×800 PNG/JPG ≤2.5 MB; carousel/app-install 1:1 |
| Focal point | "Clean images with a single center of focus"; people "medium zoom, shoulders up, or closer" | Image tips locked in an infographic (treat as unverified) |
| Text on image | "Highly recommend no ad copy or text in the image" (dynamic cropping); safe zones: 300 px per side (native stream) / 467 px center (static) | Minimal-text norm implied via rejection reasons ("poor appearance") |
| Style | "Authentic and unpolished visuals with a clear focus point"; hands-holding-product; avoid B/W | — |

Sources: Taboola Programmatic Creative Best Practices (Apr 2025), Creative Best Practices 2025 PDF, Native Stream Image Guidelines, Title/Thumbnail Policies (Apr 2025); Outbrain Ads Specs (2023 — re-verify pixel specs before publishing member docs).

### 3.2 The Columbia/Realize AI-creative study (2026)

Columbia Business School (Netzer) + Harvard/TUM/CMU on Taboola data: 300K+ ads, 3M+ clicks, 500M+ impressions. AI-generated creative performs on par with or better than human creative, and wins "by appearing authentically human and prioritizing visual trust." Per-driver CTR figures are gated behind a lead-capture download — worth requesting before writing member docs. ([Press release](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/press/ai-ads-match-human-creative-major-report-columbia-university-harvard-university), Tier 1 institution; underlying paper not yet peer-review-accessible.)

### 3.3 UGC/candid vs polished — the triangulated verdict: **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (conditional)**

- **For:** Tyrväinen et al. UGC-vs-firm-content meta-analysis (Journal of Interactive Marketing, 2023, Tier 1); Becker, Wiegand & Reinartz, "Does It Pay to Be Real?" (Journal of Marketing, 2019, 323 TV ads, Tier 1 — brand-congruent authenticity helps); Hochstein et al. digital-trust work (JAMS 2025, Tier 1); Columbia/Realize "authentically human" finding; consistent vendor doctrine (Taboola "keep it real"; agency large-N reports).
- **Against (boundary conditions):** "When AI Doesn't Sell Prada" (Journal of Advertising Research, 2025, Tier 1) — less-crafted/AI imagery *backfires* for luxury; Becker et al.'s "overly honest hurts hedonic products"; agency data showing studio creative wins for luxury/YouTube in-stream.
- **Teach:** candid, credible, single-subject imagery is the *default* for native feeds and problem-aware audiences; polish signals craft and belongs where credibility/premium positioning is the image's job (later funnel stages, high-ticket).

### 3.4 Text on image

No academic minimum-font-size-at-300px finding exists; the operative constraints are platform ones: dynamic cropping (safe zones), "no text preferred" (Taboola), and disclosure legibility (FTC, §5). Recommendation for the series: teach "no text by default; if text, 3-4 words max inside the safe zone, never load-bearing."

## 4. Stage-fit: the funnel-congruence framework (validated)

### 4.1 Academic grounding

- **Information scent** (Nielsen Norman Group, 2020, Tier 2): users follow the path whose cues best match their need; scent must stay consistent across steps or users bounce — NN/g explicitly covers why clickbait fails long-run.
- **Processing fluency / picture-text congruence:** congruent imagery raises product attitudes via fluency (Journal of Interactive Marketing, 2009, Tier 1 — note: effect moderated by need-for-cognition); Houston/Childers/Heckler 1987 (§1.2).
- **Post-click quality is enforced by platforms:** Yahoo Gemini native-ads paper (KDD 2015, Tier 1): bad post-click experience reduces future ad clicks; platforms demote low-dwell ads. Zhou et al. (WWW 2016): native-ad image features predict pre-click quality; CTR alone is a poor quality proxy.
- **Clickbait is asymmetric, not uniformly positive:** Jung, Stieglitz et al. (PLOS ONE, 2022, 4,400+ Facebook posts): some clickbait features raise engagement, others lower it. Molina et al. (ACM CHI 2021): "Does Clickbait Actually Attract More Clicks?" — mixed.
- **Source credibility → purchase intention (properly re-sourced):** Ohanian 1990 (scale: expertise/trustworthiness/attractiveness, Journal of Advertising) and Ohanian 1991 (JAR — perceived spokesperson image → purchase intention); Ismagilova et al. meta-analysis (Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services, 2020, Tier 1). This replaces the weak hospitality-sector citation in the prior internal PDF.

### 4.2 The three image jobs (synthesis — label as such in member docs)

| Stage | Image's job | Primary levers | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Native thumbnail** | Win *qualified* attention in a busy editorial feed; make a promise the next page keeps | Single focal subject; face/gaze; contrast vs white feed; bounded curiosity (positive-inference concealment); no text | Bait imagery → junk clicks → platform demotion (KDD 2015) |
| **Advertorial hero** | Confirm the promise; sustain information scent; feel editorial, not ad-like | Congruent subject/tone/palette with the thumbnail; candid, story-consistent imagery; picture-word consistency with the headline | Scent break → bounce; over-produced imagery breaks the editorial frame |
| **Jump page / pre-VSL** | Credibility and purchase-intention support | Credibility cues (expertise/trustworthiness per Ohanian lineage); product-in-context; results imagery only within compliance bounds (§5) | Hype imagery that triggers claim/compliance problems or distrust |

The harvest corroborates this observationally: all 14 Joinative full-funnel examples carry subject/tone/promise straight from thumbnail into advertorial hero (see `examples/INDEX.md`, recurring pattern #4).

**Honest caveats:** the three-jobs framework is assembled from stage-by-stage evidence, not one integrated study; "bounded curiosity" is our label — supported conceptually (post-click penalties + asymmetric clickbait effects) but no source uses the exact term; advertorial-hero evidence is the thinnest (vendor guidance + transferred congruence theory).

## 5. Compliance & rights — consolidated image no-no list

FTC doctrine frame: the Commission evaluates the **"net impression"** — "ads can communicate information through a variety of means — text, images, sounds"; an image can independently create a deceptive impression, and disclosures must sit near the ad's focal point ([FTC Native Advertising Guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/native-advertising-guide-businesses), Tier 1). **A photo implying a result (e.g., weight loss) IS a claim requiring substantiation.**

### 5.1 Banned by ALL four networks (treat as absolute)

Verified against primary policy docs: Taboola Prohibited Content (May 2025) + Health & Beauty FAQ (art. 7199777) + Thumbnail Policies (Apr 2025); Outbrain Advertising Guidelines + rejection-reasons page; MGID Compliance Guidebook §3.1 ([PDF](https://f.hubspotusercontent20.net/hubfs/5237928/Compliance_by_MGID_EN_.pdf)) + help.mgid.com; Revcontent Prohibited Content / Ad Copy Policies.

1. **Before/after photos** — explicit at Taboola ("must not show before/after photos… weight loss supplement" example), MGID ("Before/After photos" in Disallowed Image Content), Outbrain ("Before/After Photos" banned in images; landing-page before/afters require "results not typical" disclaimers), Revcontent ("fake 'before and after' transformations").
2. **Sexualized imagery / body-part emphasis** — Taboola "over emphasis on intimate body parts"; Outbrain "zoomed in body parts (unrecognizable)", cleavage; MGID "zoomed in body parts in order to sexualize"; Revcontent nudity/suggestive.
3. **Celebrities/politicians without documented permission** — Taboola (thumbnail policy, verbatim); Outbrain ("Politicians/Celebrities for Promotional purposes"); MGID ("Celebrity illegal endorsement, including politicians, scientists, doctors"); Revcontent (fabricated celebrity endorsements).
4. **Fake UI / false functionality** — Taboola "images… should not appear to… have functionality when they don't" (play buttons, close X, arrows-as-buttons); MGID "fake navigation buttons"; Revcontent bans misleading news-element imagery.
5. **Shock/gore/gross imagery** — all four (dead bodies, bodily fluids, wounds/rashes, self-harm, "grotesque").
6. **Misleading/irrelevant images** — all four; MGID: "creatives must be an accurate representation of the product"; Taboola Health FAQ: "images completely unrelated to the product… usually rejected," plus "extreme close-ups… highlighting physical features that make users feel conscious about themselves."
7. **Implied disease claims via imagery** — Taboola Health FAQ (disease vs symptom framing; hemp leaves banned in CBD thumbnails); FTC substantiation doctrine backs this generally.

### 5.2 Where networks genuinely differ (check per network)

- **Arrows/circles overlays:** Outbrain bans outright; MGID bans "elements placed to highlight a specific part of the thumbnail"; **Revcontent conditionally allows** ("arrows or circles for emphasis when appropriate," banning "poorly drawn or distracting" ones); Taboola is silent except the false-functionality clause.
- **Animated images:** banned at MGID; format-dependent elsewhere.
- **Money imagery:** MGID explicitly bans "large amount of money rolls or bills"; others catch it under misleading/unrealistic-expectations clauses.

### 5.3 Rights & licensing

- **Editorial-use-only stock cannot be used in ads/advertorials** — Shutterstock license, verbatim restriction 05 (primary, fetched Jul 2026); Getty/iStock carry equivalent terms (their exact current wording not re-pulled — verify before member docs).
- **People photos in ads need model releases**; using a real person's likeness commercially without consent risks right-of-publicity claims (e.g., Cal. Civ. Code §3344, fee-shifting).
- **AI-likeness law (fast-moving):** NY synthetic-performer disclosure in commercial ads effective **June 9, 2026**; NY posthumous right of publicity (Dec 2025); California AB 2602/AB 1836 (2024); Tennessee ELVIS Act (2024). Federal **NO FAKES Act** cleared Senate Judiciary June 2026 but is not law as of this report. Taboola's Generative-AI disclaimer (2023) pushes all liability to the advertiser; no network yet publishes a specific AI-people image rule.
- **Practical rule for members:** never use a recognizable real person you don't have a release for; disclose AI-generated "people" where required (NY reach is broad); prefer licensed commercial stock or original/AI imagery of non-identifiable people, and keep records.

## 7. Limitations & open questions

1. **Native-feed specificity:** almost no Tier-1 study tests Taboola/Outbrain/MGID/Revcontent thumbnails directly; evidence transfers from print, banner, and YouTube-thumbnail research. Reasonable inference, flagged as such.
2. **Curiosity-gap image isolation:** concealment experiments (JM 2020) are lab/preference studies; no live-feed experiment isolates image-borne curiosity from the headline.
3. **Stage-fit is a synthesis** — no single integrated three-stage study; advertorial-hero evidence is the thinnest leg.
4. **Columbia/Realize per-driver CTR numbers** are gated behind a lead-capture download; worth obtaining before writing member docs.
5. **Quantified subject-background separation and thumbnail text-legibility thresholds** don't exist in the academic literature; only platform doctrine covers them.
6. **Undated network policy pages** (Outbrain guidelines, Revcontent help) should be re-fetched at authoring time; Outbrain image-tips infographic remains machine-unreadable.
7. **Getty/iStock current license text** not re-pulled (Shutterstock verified as primary); confirm before publishing licensing guidance.
8. **Gaze meta-analysis gap:** no clean meta-analysis maps direct-vs-averted gaze to specific objectives; the tradeoff rule is assembled from individual Tier-1 studies.

## 8. Proposed curriculum outline — Image Foundations member series

> **PROPOSAL ONLY** — for review before any member-doc work begins. Mirrors the Copywriting Foundations format.

1. **Why images are stage-dependent** — the three image jobs (thumbnail / advertorial hero / jump page); information scent; why the click promise must be kept.
2. **The attention toolkit** — faces & gaze direction (direct vs aimed); single focal subject; shoulders-up crops; complexity sweet spot at thumbnail size.
3. **Bounded curiosity** — partial reveal done right; the positive-inference rule; why bait imagery destroys accounts, not just campaigns.
4. **Color without the folklore** — saturation & potency; contrast-vs-feed; congruence over hue; the myths to unlearn.
5. **Candid vs polished** — when raw wins (native feeds, problem-aware) and when polish wins (credibility stage, premium offers).
6. **Congruence through the funnel** — carrying subject/tone/promise from thumbnail to advertorial to jump page; picture-headline consistency.
7. **The compliance gauntlet** — the cross-network no-no list; images-as-claims (FTC); network differences worth knowing.
8. **Rights, releases, and AI people** — stock licensing traps (editorial-use-only), model releases, AI-likeness disclosure rules.
9. **Building your image library** — sourcing, recreating (not republishing) reference creatives, keeping records.
