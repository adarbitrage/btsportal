## Recurring visual patterns observed
1. **One clear focal subject wins at every stage.** Winning thumbnails and advertorial heroes almost always center a single person, face, dish, or product — never a cluttered collage. Taboola states this explicitly ("clear focus point", "shoulders-up people").
2. **People + emotional congruence.** Faces/couples/lifestyle shots dominate; the emotion in the image matches the promise (happy couples for dating, appetizing food for diet apps). Authentic/unpolished beats over-produced stock.
3. **Curiosity or specificity in the headline paired with a matching image.** Question hooks ("Why is it never too late to love?", "Are we heading for WWIII?") and specific numbers ("300+ calories, 10-min") recur — and the vendors stress the *image must set accurate expectations* so the click isn't a bait-and-switch.
4. **Funnel congruence is the through-line.** The strongest examples carry the thumbnail's subject, tone, and promise straight into the advertorial/LP hero (same imagery, same claim). Broken congruence is implicitly the cautionary pattern.
5. **Advertorial/jump pages borrow editorial trust.** MGID + Joinative advertorials mimic blog/magazine layouts (bylines, "ADVERTORIAL" labels, press-logo bars, first-person testimonials) — effective, but the heaviest-claim finance/health examples are exactly where compliance/rights review matters most.
