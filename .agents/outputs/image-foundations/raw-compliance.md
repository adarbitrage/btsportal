## Key Facts

**Native network image policies**
- Taboola prohibits ads that "misrepresent" via imagery: "Images and text should not appear to or suggest that they have functionality, when they don't" — this is the rule against fake UI (play buttons, arrows, close/X buttons) that imply interactivity (taboola.com/help/en/articles/3878202, updated May 29, 2025).
- Taboola bans "over emphasis on intimate body parts," covered nudity, obscene imagery (dead bodies, bodily fluids, feces), and shock/scare tactics — including creating "a false sense of urgency… through language or images" (same URL).
- Taboola treats implied claims as claims: an ad may not "set unrealistic expectations"; supplement ads implying disease treatment/reversal are prohibited even if via imagery/context (same URL).
- Outbrain (now under Teads brand) explicitly bans in ad images: "Circles or Arrows placed to highlight a specific part of the image," "Zoomed in body parts," "Before/After Photos," "Politicians/Celebrities for Promotional purposes," violence/gore/dead bodies, graphic physical deformities/health conditions, and sexually suggestive imagery (outbrain.com/advertisers/guidelines/).
- Outbrain rejection reasons name specific image faults: "Suggestive or Inappropriate" (nudity incl. cleavage), "Overly Zoomed In (Unrecognizable)" body parts, and "Poor Appearance" = blurriness, incorrect cropping, or "Photoshopped elements" (outbrain.com/help/advertisers/why-was-my-content-rejected/).
- Outbrain requires "Results may vary/not typical" disclaimers next to any before/after images on the landing page for health offers (same guidelines URL).
- Revcontent prohibits "before and after" images tied to unverified health/weight-loss claims, fabricated/fake celebrity endorsements, shock/scare tactics, nudity/suggestive imagery, and "misleading use of news elements" (fake reporter images, national-outlet logos/layouts on landing pages) (help.revcontent.com/knowledge/prohibited-content; .../why-is-my-ad-denied).
- Revcontent DIFFERS on arrows/circles: its Ad Copy spec permits "arrows or circles for emphasis when appropriate," but bans "poorly drawn or distracting arrows/circles," "overly edited or heavily photoshopped" images, collage-style images, grotesque imagery (blood/rashes/torture), and "fake 'before and after' transformations" (help.revcontent.com/knowledge/ad-copy-policies).

**FTC (native advertising / deceptive format)**
- FTC evaluates the "net impression" of an ad and looks at the "overall context," explicitly including images: "ads can communicate information through a variety of means – text, images, sounds." Visual elements can independently create a deceptive impression (ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/native-advertising-guide-businesses).
- Disclosures must appear near the ad's "focal point" (e.g., near the headline/image where attention lands) and be clear and conspicuous within the native format (same URL).
- Deceptive-format doctrine: content that mimics the surrounding editorial "written, spoken, or visual style or subject matter" of the publisher may be deceptive as to its commercial nature — directly relevant to advertorial/jump-page imagery (same URL). This implies weight-loss/results imagery IS a claim requiring substantiation.

**Stock licensing / rights of people**
- "Editorial use only" stock content may NOT be used in advertising/advertorials. iStock license: "You may not use content marked 'editorial use only' for any commercial, promotional, advertorial, endorsement, advertising… purpose." Such content is "not model or property released" (via vondranlegal.com/editorial-images-can-get-you-sued, citing iStock/Getty/Shutterstock/Alamy terms).
- Commercial use of a person's photo generally requires a model release; using a person's/celebrity's image for marketing risks a right-of-publicity claim (e.g., California Civil Code §3344, with attorney-fee recovery for the prevailing party) (same URL).

**AI-generated likeness / right of publicity (2024–2026)**
- New York (S.8391/A.8882, signed Dec 11, 2025): (a) posthumous right of publicity — requires prior consent from a deceased personality's heirs/executors for commercial use of name/voice/image/likeness (effective immediately Dec 11, 2025); (b) AI-transparency law requiring "conspicuous" disclosure of AI-generated "synthetic performers" (digitally created media appearing as a real but non-identifiable person) in commercial ads, with civil penalties — effective June 9, 2026 (debevoise.com Dec 17, 2025 update).
- California (signed Sept 17, 2024): AB 2602 voids contracts allowing AI "digital replicas" of a performer's voice/likeness without specified consent/representation; AB 1836 bars commercial use of a deceased personality's digital replica in expressive works without estate consent (leginfo.legislature.ca.gov AB-2602 & AB-1836; gov.ca.gov/2024/09/17).
- Tennessee ELVIS Act (Public Chapter 588 / HB 2091, 2024): adds "voice" to protected likeness and creates liability for making available AI tools whose primary purpose is producing unauthorized likeness/voice replicas (internetandtechnologylaw.com/files/2024/07/ELVIS-Act.pdf).

## Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference
- Arrows/circles: Outbrain bans them outright in images; Revcontent permits them "for emphasis when appropriate." - The specific "fake play button / X / close button" prohibition is stated verbatim only via Taboola's "false functionality" principle; other networks imply it under "misleading/deceptive" but do not enumerate button types. Cross-reference each network's current misrepresentation clause.
