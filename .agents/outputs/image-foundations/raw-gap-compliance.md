## Findings per hole

### HOLE 1 — MGID explicit IMAGE rules (PRIMARY docs verified)

**MGID Compliance Guidebook (EN PDF), §3.1.2 "Disallowed Image Content"** — verbatim prohibited image list:
> "Adult content · Aggressive image design · Animated images · **Before/After photos** · **Body parts that look undefinable** · **Celebrity illegal endorsement, including politicians, scientists, doctors, etc.** · Copyright infringement · **Fake navigation buttons** · **Gross imagery** · **Hidden or exposed nudity** · Images depicting a large amount money rolls or bills · Images of minors for mature topics · Irelevancy [sic] · Minors sexualizing · **Misleading messaging** · Mocking materials · **Optical illusions** · Religious symbols (Might be acceptable if used for charity… reviewed individually) · Self-harming · Violence and gore · **Zoomed in body parts in order to sexualize the image**"
Source: f.hubspotusercontent20.net/hubfs/5237928/Compliance_by_MGID_EN_.pdf, p.21–22.

**Same PDF, §3.1.1 Do's/Don'ts** — DON'Ts include: "Disgusting concepts · Disturbing conditions · **Elements placed to highlight a specific part of the thumbnail** · Sexual subject matter." Relevancy rule: *"Creatives must be an accurate representation of the product or service advertised… Using false tactics to incentivize is prohibited."*

**§4 Specific Content & Creative Restrictions** shows worked non-compliant examples labeled as violations — e.g. weight-loss thumbnails flagged **"Display of naked body parts"/"Display of nudity"** (belly-fat, "98 Kg to 56 Kg" before/after imagery), and note that *"each component of the non-compliant ad unit… is considered non-compliant even if being used in different combinations."*

**help.mgid.com/prohibited-imagery** (publisher-facing, permanent-ban list): *"Violence (shooting, tragedies, sexual assault, injuring, self-harm) · Adult · Dead bodies · Defecation scenes · Nudity · Racy · Strobing · Intolerance · Discrimination."*
No AI-image rule exists in any MGID guidebook (EN/India/LATAM) — "artificial intelligence" appears only as a *content category*, not a creative rule.

### HOLE 2 — Taboola (Realize) health/supplement IMAGE rules (PRIMARY docs verified)

**Policy FAQ – Health & Beauty** (taboola.com/help/en/articles/7199777): images "usually rejected":
> "**Unrealistic or photoshopped before and after images** · Images completely unrelated to the product (especially when the headline implies the product is in the image) · **Unpleasant or inappropriate imagery (especially in the thumbnail!) — Example: Extreme close-ups, suggestive images, highlighting physical features that make users feel conscious about themselves**." Implied-disease rule: "Claims about diseases or conditions instead of symptoms (Cancer/diabetes instead of pain/fatigue)"; absolute cure/treat/reverse claims prohibited. CBD: "hemp leaves are also prohibited in thumbnails."

**Title, Thumbnail and Branding Text Policies** (article 3878208, dated Apr 27 2025) — Thumbnail Image Policies, verbatim:
> "Thumbnails must not be misleading… · **Thumbnails must not show before/after photos.** Example: A thumbnail for a weight loss supplement campaign must not use a photo of an individual before taking the supplement alongside another photo… after… · Thumbnails must not be of poor quality/very low resolution · **Thumbnails must not feature celebrities or politicians when the campaign promotes a product or service, unless you can produce documented permission from the individuals featured.**"

### HOLE 3 — Stock-license PRIMARY terms (Shutterstock verified)

**Shutterstock TOS/License** (shutterstock.com/license) — Restrictions, verbatim:
> "05. **Use Visual Content designated 'Editorial Use Only'… for commercial purposes, including… in any advertising, merchandise or other non-editorial contexts.**" (prohibited) — confirms (a).
> "02. Portray any person ('Model') in a way a reasonable person would find offensive, including… **d) as suffering from, or medicating for, a physical or mental ailment; a) in connection with… dating services**…" — confirms (c): health/dating uses restricted. Also "03. Use… in a defamatory or deceptive context."

**Shutterstock "Why is content Editorial Use Only" help** (dated May 19 2026): editorial = "content we don't have the legal release to license for commercial purposes"; triggers include **"It portrays a celebrity — almost always Editorial Use Only"** and **"It portrays a person without a consent form… can't be used for commercial purposes."** "Commercial use is any promotion/advertising for a product or service that generates revenue." → confirms (b) model/consent release required for commercial people photos.

### HOLE 4 — Federal AI-likeness status + native-network AI rules (verified)

**NO FAKES Act — status mid-2026:** Reintroduced as **S. 4591, "NO FAKES Act of 2026"**; **unanimously advanced (voice vote) out of the Senate Judiciary Committee on June 18, 2026**; **"now moves to the full Senate for approval"** (Holland & Knight alert, June 22 2026; Blackburn Senate press release June 2026). Creates a federal IP right in voice/visual likeness against unauthorized AI "digital replicas." **Not yet law.** (Earlier vehicle S.1367, 119th Congress, still shows status "Introduced" only — congress.gov.)

**Native network published AI-in-image rules:**
- **Outbrain (operating under Teads brand)** — Advertising Guidelines have an explicit **"AI Generated or Modified Content Guidelines"** section (PRIMARY, strongest): *"All advertising content generated or materially modified using AI… must be clearly labelled as AI-generated at the point of first exposure. **Advertisements featuring synthetic likenesses, voice clones, or AI-manipulated representations of real persons shall be prohibited unless the advertiser holds explicit consent from the individual depicted and includes a disclosure making clear that the person shown or heard is not genuine.** Machine-readable provenance markers… must not be removed or suppressed. AI shall not be used to fabricate testimonials, simulate user reviews… or produce synthetic 'before and after' imagery."*
- **Taboola** — "Generative AI for Ads Disclaimer" (policies.taboola.com, last updated Jul 1 2023): AI output = "Advertiser Content"; Taboola "will not take any steps to check whether such content infringes third-party… rights"; advertiser is "fully responsible… complies with all applicable laws (including advertising regulations)" and indemnifies Taboola. No specific AI-people/likeness image rule found in Taboola advertiser policies.
- **MGID / Revcontent** — no published rule specific to AI-generated people in ad images found (MGID guidebooks silent; Revcontent's AI docs cover RevGPT headlines only).
