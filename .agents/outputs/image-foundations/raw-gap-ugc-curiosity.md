## Claim A findings — "Candid/UGC-style imagery outperforms polished stock in feed ads"

Independent support exists but is heavily CONDITIONAL, not a blanket rule:
- **Becker, Wiegand & Reinartz, "Does It Pay to Be Real?" (Journal of Marketing, 2019, Tier 1)** — 323 TV ads / 67 brands / 4 years. Authenticity's effect is *nuanced*: brand-congruent authenticity helps sales, but "overly honest" ad messaging can *hurt* performance, especially for hedonic products. So authenticity is not uniformly beneficial.
- **Tyrväinen et al. meta-analysis, "UGC vs. Firm-Generated Content" (Journal of Interactive Marketing, 2023, Tier 1)** — independent meta-analytic evidence that UGC generally outperforms firm-generated content on engagement/loyalty outcomes (moderated by product type). Confirms directionally, at scale, independent of Taboola.
- Agency/vendor large-N (Ads&Scale, SaliencyLab, adspyder — Tier 3): consistently report UGC wins on TikTok/DTC/wellness lower-funnel, but studio wins for YouTube in-stream and luxury, and "inflated UGC" penalizes premium brands. Directionally corroborative but non-independent/non-peer-reviewed.

COUNTER-evidence (polished/professional wins):
- **"When AI Doesn't Sell Prada" (Journal of Advertising Research, 2025, Vol 65(2), Tier 1, 24 citations)** — 3 experiments (field + lab): AI-generated/less-crafted imagery *backfires* for luxury brands, damaging brand perception. Polish/craft signals credibility at the high-price/luxury tier.
- The JM 2019 hedonic-product finding above is itself a boundary condition on the "keep it raw" doctrine.

## Claim B findings — "Curiosity-gap applies to IMAGES, not just headlines"

Now DIRECTLY supported by peer-reviewed causal experiments, no longer inferential:
- **Sevilla, Zhang & Kahn (implied authorship), "Leaving Something for the Imagination: The Effect of Visual Concealment on Preferences" (Journal of Marketing, 2020, Tier 1, 57 citations)** — SIX experiments. The IMAGE itself (partial/concealed view) arouses curiosity, which transfers positive affect and inflates preference/choice likelihood vs. showing the full image. This is the image-as-curiosity-gap mechanism proven experimentally. Key boundary: works only when initial inferences are positive ("products that benefit most have the least to hide") — an important post-click-quality caveat.
- **Collins & Litman, "The measurement of perceptual curiosity" (Personality & Individual Differences, 2003, Tier 1, 263 citations)** — establishes perceptual (visual/sensory) curiosity as a distinct construct driven by novel/ambiguous perceptual stimuli, grounding the mechanism.
- **Xie, Le & Lee, "CHECKER: Detecting Clickbait Thumbnails" (ECML-PKDD 2021, Tier 2)** — defines visual clickbait as deceptive/sensationalized/exaggerating thumbnail images that entice clicks but whose landing content is *inconsistent* — i.e., visual bait harming post-click quality (the "click AND post-click quality" angle).
- **Zhou, Redi, Haines & Lalmas (Yahoo Labs), "Predicting Pre-click Quality for Native Advertisements" (WWW 2016, Tier 1/2)** — native-ad image (plus text/advertiser) features predict user experience; shows CTR alone is a poor quality proxy vs. offensive-feedback rate. Directly supports measuring both click and post-click quality of native-ad imagery.
- Supporting: "Knowledge Gap Illustrations Spark Curiosity" (Journal of Cognition, 2025) and Liao "Occluded Text as an Attention/Preference Cue" (SAGE Open, 2023) — occlusion/partial-reveal visual cues raise attention/curiosity.

## Verdicts
- **Claim A: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.** Independent Tier-1 evidence (JM 2019; JIM 2023 meta-analysis) confirms UGC/authenticity often outperforms — but it is context-dependent, not universal. Strong Tier-1 counter-evidence (JAR 2025 Prada; JM 2019 hedonic caveat) shows polished wins for luxury/high-price/credibility contexts. The unqualified "raw always beats polished" framing is closer to vendor folklore; the *conditional* version is supported.
- **Claim B: SUPPORTED.** No longer inferential. JM 2020 visual-concealment experiments directly demonstrate the image creating a curiosity gap that drives preference; Collins/Litman ground perceptual curiosity; CHECKER + Zhou et al. characterize visual bait and its post-click-quality risk. The important nuance: visual curiosity gaps lift clicks/preference only when underlying inferences/payoff are positive — otherwise they degrade post-click quality (clickbait).
