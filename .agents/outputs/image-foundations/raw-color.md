## Key Facts

- **Saturation → perceived potency/efficacy replicates robustly.** Labrecque et al. (2024, *Journal of Marketing*, "Color Me Effective") establishes across **7 lab experiments (n=2,745), a web-scraping study, and a field experiment** that higher color saturation makes consumers infer greater product efficacy/potency. Crucially, consumers *overgeneralize* this beyond the product's own color to **packaging color AND the background color used in advertisements** — direct relevance to ad imagery. Effect held across 6 hues with lightness constant; boundary condition: high saturation *lowers* perceived efficacy when consumers want a "gentle" product. (https://doi.org/10.1177/00222429241296392 ; open PDF: https://findresearcher.sdu.dk/ws/files/303255637/Labrecque-et-al-2024_Color-me-effective...pdf)
- **Saturation drives perceived size/heft/strength — more predictive than hue.** Hagtvedt & Brasel (2017, *JCR*) show saturated products are perceived larger/heavier; "attention-grabbing" is a saturation effect, not a specific-hue effect. (https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/44/2/396/3061448)
- **Color meaning is context-dependent, not universal (color-in-context theory).** Elliot (2015 review, *Frontiers in Psychology*) and Elliot & Maier (2014, *Annual Review of Psychology*) conclude color effects on cognition/behavior vary by task, product category, and culturally-learned meaning; the field is "nascent," urging "patience and prudence" about real-world application. There is **no universal "color = attribute" map.** (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00368/pdf ; https://psycnet.apa.org/record/2013-40122-007)
- **"Red = urgency/sales" is bidirectional, not universal.** Bagchi & Cheema (2013, *JCR*): red *increases* aggressive bids in competitive auctions but *reduces* offers in 1:1 negotiations. Retail attention from "red" is an artifact of saturation/warmth (same for saturated orange/yellow), not the hue red. (https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/39/5/947/1792819)
- **"Blue = trust" fails as a universal; congruence is the real driver.** Labrecque & Milne (2012, *JAMS*, "Exciting Red and Competent Blue," 208 brands mapped): **consistency between brand color and perceived brand personality predicts preference/purchase better than color in absolute terms.** A "competent" brand performs similarly in blue, dark green, or charcoal — internal consistency matters, not the specific hue. (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-010-0245-y)
- **The "62–90% of judgment is color" stat is misused.** It traces to Singh (2006, *Management Decision*), which described *initial visual judgment* (color as one of several dominant components alongside shape/layout/typography) — NOT a color-to-sales correlation. Widely misrepresented in vendor blogs. (secondary: deepmarketing.it evidence review)
- **Contrast-against-environment beats specific hue (thumbnails).** Matt Heisig (HitFactor), analyzing **1.53 million YouTube videos**, publicly reversed his prior advice: *"I used to tell clients to crank the color and contrast… Then I tested 1.53 million videos. I was wrong."* Practitioner-scale corroboration that maxing saturation/contrast is not a universal win; salience is relative to surrounding feed, not absolute. (https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mheisig_hitfactor-labs-005-color-contrast-in-youtube-activity-7474900752197496833-A4-F)
- **Native ads in editorial (white) feeds — supporting evidence on contrast/congruence.** Banner-ad eye-tracking (MDPI, *Future Internet* 2021) and color–product congruence eye-tracking work show attention is governed by relevance/contrast and design fit rather than any single hue; product–background color combination (similar vs. contrastive) measurably shifts product evaluations (Huang 2020, *Psychology & Marketing*). (https://www.mdpi.com/1999-5903/13/1/18 ; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mar.21361)

## Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference

- **Heisig 1.53M-video finding** is a single practitioner/vendor (HitFactor) LinkedIn source; the underlying dataset/methodology is not peer-reviewed or public (LinkedIn URL returned 403; finding captured via search index). Direction (contrast is relative, not "always crank it") is *consistent* with academic saliency literature and Labrecque's boundary conditions, but the specific magnitude/claims should be flagged as un-refereed.
- **"Su et al. (2019) Trustworthy Blue vs Untrustworthy Red"** (*J. Marketing Theory & Practice*) appears to support a blue-trust effect — this *conflicts* on the surface with the "no universal color" consensus. Likely reconcilable via context-dependence, but I did not fetch the full text; cross-reference before citing either way. (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/334550253)
- **Afonso & Janiszewski (2023, *J. Marketing*)** cited (via secondary source) for "CTR predicted by contrast + button shape, not hue." I did not reach the primary; verify title/finding directly before using in training.
- The 2024 Labrecque paper is **15 citations / very recent** — replications by *independent* labs are still limited; internal replication (9 studies) is strong but external replication is nascent.

## Gaps & Unanswered Questions

- **No direct study found on native-feed thumbnails specifically** (Taboola/Outbrain/MGID/Revcontent) on white editorial pages. Evidence is transferred from adjacent domains (YouTube thumbnails, display/banner eye-tracking, product–background contrast). This transfer is reasonable but should be labeled as inference, not native-specific proof.
- **Independent replication of Labrecque 2024** (outside the author team) not yet established.
- **Interaction of saturation × contrast-against-white-background** in a feed context is untested directly — Labrecque shows saturated *ad backgrounds* raise efficacy perception, while Heisig warns against maxing contrast; how these reconcile in a white-page native feed is an open question worth flagging for the training series.
- Su et al. (2019) blue-trust vs. the no-universal consensus not fully reconciled (full text not read).
- Cross-cultural stability of any of these effects for affiliate audiences is under-documented.

**Bottom line for the training series:** What replicates = (a) *saturation → perceived potency/efficacy*, and it transfers to ad backgrounds (Labrecque 2024; Hagtvedt & Brasel 2017); (b) *color–brand/personality congruence* beats "magic hues" (Labrecque & Milne 2012); (c) *color meaning is context-dependent* (Elliot & Maier). Myth = universal "red=urgency / blue=trust" — the same hue flips direction by context (Bagchi & Cheema). For native feeds, prioritize *contrast/differentiation against the surrounding white editorial environment and category norms* over any specific hue — but avoid blindly "cranking" contrast (Heisig's reversal + Labrecque's gentle-product boundary).
