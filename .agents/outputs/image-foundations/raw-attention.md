## Key Facts

**Gaze-cueing (replication status: robust core effect, but two competing mechanisms)**
- Palcu, Sudkamp & Florack (Frontiers in Psychology, 2017) — eye-tracking, fictional online shopping page with banners. When a face gazed toward the product, viewers were more likely to look at the product (regardless of static/animated), AND gaze-toward-product raised purchase intention vs. gaze-away. Animation captured attention but gaze direction is what transferred attention to the product and shaped intention. This is the requested "gaze-cuing banner" study. (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.00881/full)
- Independent replication (print): Hutton & Nolte, "The effect of gaze cues on attention to print advertisements" (Applied Cognitive Psychology, 2011) — gaze cues reliably direct viewer attention within the ad; a face looking at the product pulls fixations to the product. (https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.1763)
- Independent replication (eye-tracking, neuro): D'Ambrogio, Werksman, Platt & Johnson, "How celebrity status and gaze direction in ads drive visual attention to shape consumer decisions" (Psychology & Marketing, April 2023). N=77, eye-tracking + pupillometry + drift-diffusion modeling. Both gaze-cueing and celebrity endorsement shifted gaze allocation and biased choice (celebrity shifted the starting point/rate of evidence accumulation). Strong recent, mechanistic replication of gaze-cueing in ads. (https://doi.org/10.1002/mar.21772 ; abstract at https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1560464)
- THE DIRECT-vs-AVERTED TRADEOFF: (a) Direct gaze / gaze-toward-product = attention transfer + connection; (b) To & Patrick, "How the Eyes Connect to the Heart: The Influence of Eye Gaze Direction on Advertising Effectiveness" (Journal of Consumer Research, 2021, vol. 48(1):123-146) argue AVERTED gaze can increase ad effectiveness via immersion/transportation — the viewer follows the model's gaze into the scene and becomes more absorbed. This is the requested JCR 2021 source and is the key tension: direct gaze builds interpersonal connection/trust; averted gaze (toward the product/CTA) transfers attention and can deepen immersion. (https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/jconrs/v48y2021i1p123-146..html ; DOI 10.1093/jcr/ucaa063)

**Faces: attention capture vs. distraction from product/CTA**
- Faces reliably capture attention and combat "banner blindness" (Palcu et al. 2017, above). BUT a face left to itself holds fixations ON the face — potentially stealing attention from product/CTA. The resolution in the literature: the face captures the first fixation, and its GAZE DIRECTION then redirects attention to the product. Adil, Lacoste-Badie & Droulers, "Face Presence and Gaze Direction in Print Advertisements" (Journal of Advertising Research, 2018) — eye-tracking confirms faces attract attention and gaze direction steers it to the target; face without directional cue risks capturing attention without transferring it. (https://doi.org/10.2501/jar-2018-004)
- Xiao & Ding, "Just the Faces: Exploring the Effects of Facial Features in Print Advertising" (Marketing Science, 2014) — facial features in ads systematically affect brand outcomes; faces are a strong, measurable attention/response lever. (https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2013.0837)

**Picture-superiority effect & picture-word consistency (JMR 1987 lineage)**
- Houston, Childers & Heckler, "Picture-Word Consistency and the Elaborative Processing of Advertisements" (Journal of Marketing Research, 1987, 24(4)) — the requested JMR 1987 primary source. When picture and words are CONSISTENT, elaborative processing and memory improve; inconsistency disrupts encoding. Supports picture-word congruence for the advertorial hero/thumbnail. (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/002224378702400403)
- Childers & Houston, "Memory for the visual and verbal components of print advertisements" (Psychology & Marketing, 1986) — establishes picture-superiority in the ad context: pictures are remembered better than words (dual-coding lineage, Paivio). (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mar.4220030303)
- Nuance / boundary condition: Houston, Childers & Heckler and later Heckler & Childers, "The Role of Expectancy and Relevancy in Memory for Verbal and Visual Information: What is Incongruency?" (JCR, 1992) — RELEVANT incongruity (surprising but on-topic) can boost memory, whereas irrelevant incongruity hurts. Relevant to curiosity-gap imagery. (https://doi.org/10.1086/209275)

**Curiosity gap applied to IMAGES / thumbnails**
- Loewenstein, "The Psychology of Curiosity: A Review and Reinterpretation" (Psychological Bulletin, 1994, 116(1):75-98) — THE primary source for information-gap theory (curiosity = aversive feeling from a gap between what one knows and wants to know; a small, closable gap maximizes curiosity). Correctly replaces the prior Cambridge-handbook citation. (https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf ; DOI 10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75)
- IMPORTANT COUNTER-EVIDENCE on curiosity-gap tactics: Molina, Sundar, Rony, Hassan, Le & Lee, "Does Clickbait Actually Attract More Clicks? Three Clickbait Studies You Must Read" (ACM CHI 2021) — across three studies, headlines algorithmically classified as "clickbait" did NOT reliably generate more curiosity or clicks; classifiers agreed only 47% of the time. Tempering claim: crude curiosity-gap tactics don't reliably work; the gap must be genuine and closable. (https://pike.psu.edu/publications/chi21.pdf ; DOI 10.1145/3411764.3445753)
- Thumbnail-image research (visual, not headline): "Who Wants to Be a Click-Millionaire? On the Influence of Thumbnails and Captions" (ICPR 2022) and "Visual Attributes of Thumbnails in Predicting YouTube Brand Channel Views" (IEEE Trans. Computational Social Systems, 2023) — thumbnail visual attributes measurably predict view-through; faces, and specific visual features drive selection. (https://doi.org/10.1109/icpr56361.2022.9956202 ; https://doi.org/10.1109/tcss.2023.3289410)

**Inverted-U visual complexity ↔ attention**
- "The dynamic effects of visual complexity and scene cuts on viewer attention" (Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science, published online 26 March 2025, DOI 10.1007/s11747-025-01137-x) — the requested JAMS 2025 source; models how visual complexity dynamically affects attention. (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-025-01137-x)
- "Visual complexity, brand gender, and ad effectiveness" (International Journal of Research in Marketing, 2025, 42(2)) — visual complexity's effect on ad effectiveness is moderated (nonlinear/contingent), consistent with an inverted-U rather than "more/less is always better." (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167811624000867)

**Universal facial expressions — corrected academic standing**
- Original claim (universal basic emotions) traces to Ekman & Friesen's cross-cultural work (1970s). Post-2015 critique: Gendron, Crivelli & Barrett, "Universality Reconsidered: Diversity in Making Meaning of Facial Expressions" (Current Directions in Psychological Science, 2018, 27(4):211-219, DOI 10.1177/0963721417746794) — argues emotion perception from faces shows substantial CULTURAL VARIATION and the strong universality thesis is not supported when free-labeling (vs. forced-choice) methods are used. (https://affective-science.org/pubs/2018/gendron-et-al-universality-cdps.pdf) Also Gendron et al., "Cultural Variation in Emotion Perception Is Real: A Response to Sauter, Eisner, Ekman & Scott (2015)" (Psychological Science, DOI 10.1177/0956797614566659). Correct framing for the training: facial-emotion signaling is powerful and partly shared, but NOT strictly universal — it's culturally moderated.

## Notable Claims Requiring Cross-Reference
- **Averted vs. direct gaze is genuinely conflicting across sources.** Palcu 2017 / Adil 2018 / D'Ambrogio 2023 emphasize gaze-cueing toward product transfers attention; To & Patrick 2021 (JCR) show averted gaze boosts immersion, while a large gaze-cueing literature and folk practice favor direct gaze for connection/trust. Resolution likely depends on GOAL (connection/trust = direct gaze at viewer; attention transfer to product/CTA = gaze averted toward the product). Flag as a "it depends on funnel stage" principle, not a universal rule.
- **Curiosity-gap effectiveness for images** is under-replicated. Loewenstein 1994 is theory; Molina et al. 2021 shows headline clickbait underperforms expectations. Direct experimental evidence that a curiosity-gap IMAGE (vs. headline) drives clicks is thin — most is correlational thumbnail-prediction work (ICPR 2022, IEEE TCSS 2023). Treat visual curiosity gap as plausible-but-not-strongly-replicated.
- **Inverted-U specifically** (as opposed to "complexity is moderated/nonlinear") — the 2025 JAMS/IJRM papers support nonlinearity and moderation; verify the exact inverted-U functional form in the JAMS full text before asserting a clean peak.

## Gaps & Unanswered Questions
- No study found that directly tests native-ad (Taboola/Outbrain/MGID/Revcontent) thumbnails specifically; all evidence is print, banner, or YouTube thumbnail — generalization to native feeds is an inference (reasonable but unverified).
- Direct experimental evidence for a curiosity-gap IMAGE driving clicks (isolating the image from the headline) is essentially missing; thumbnail work is predictive/correlational.
- The averted-vs-direct gaze tradeoff lacks a clean meta-analysis mapping WHICH condition wins for WHICH objective (attention transfer vs. trust) — a true meta-analysis/replication registry was not located.
- Exact functional form of the visual-complexity/attention curve (strict inverted-U vs. threshold vs. context-dependent) needs the JAMS 2025 full text to confirm.
- Faces-steal-attention: no source cleanly quantifies CTA fixation loss when a non-cueing face is present vs. absent; inferred from gaze-transfer studies.
