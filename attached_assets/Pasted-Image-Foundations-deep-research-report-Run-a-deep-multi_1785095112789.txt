A prior condensed agent pass, whose findings are summarized here — treat these as established starting points:
Gaze cueing is well-replicated (Frontiers in Psychology 2017 gaze-cuing banner study; "How the Eyes Connect to the Heart," Journal of Consumer Research 2021): a model's gaze steers attention toward what it points at; direct gaze builds connection, averted gaze transfers attention. Faces capture attention disproportionately but can steal attention from the product.
Picture-superiority effect has decades of support; picture-word consistency matters (JMR 1987).
Visual complexity has an inverted-U relationship with attention (JAMS 2025).
Most pop color psychology ("red = urgency, blue = trust") is weakly supported. What replicates: saturation → perceived potency/arousal (Labrecque et al. 2024); color-claim congruence; and contrast against the feed environment beats any specific hue (a 1.53M-video YouTube thumbnail study by Matt Heisig/HitFactor found max-saturation advice wrong — differentiation wins). Native feeds are mostly white editorial pages, so this translates directly.
Platform doctrine (Taboola/Outbrain official): single focal point, tight crops, minimal/no text-on-image, high subject-background separation; candid/editorial/UGC-style photos outperform polished stock — but that last claim currently rests entirely on Tier-3 vendor sources and NEEDS triangulation.
Key primary documents surfaced but not yet parsed in full: Taboola Creative Best Practices 2025 PDF (taboola.com/wp-content/uploads-neo/2025/04/Creative-Best-Practices-2025-_-Taboola-Creative-Shop.pdf — includes a Columbia-led AI-vs-human creative study), Taboola 40 Native Advertising Examples ebook (taboola.com/documents/taboola-40-native-advertising-examples-ebook.pdf), Taboola health-supplement advertising guidelines PDF, Taboola prohibited-content policy (taboola.com/help/en/articles/3878202), Outbrain image/creative best practices, Joinative Native Advertising Lookbook (joinative.com/assets/pdf/Native-Advertising-Lookbook.pdf), FTC native advertising guide (ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/native-advertising-guide-businesses).
Compliance no-no list so far (to be consolidated and expanded): fake UI elements (play buttons, arrows, close buttons); sexualized imagery/intimate body-part emphasis; shock/gore/dead bodies/bodily fluids; false urgency through imagery; before/after body-transformation photos on health offers; celebrity/person images without permission; brand logos without permission; significantly altered images and image–landing-page mismatch; FTC deceptive-format doctrine; editorial-use-only stock licenses in ads; AI-generated likenesses of real people (2025–26 NY right-of-publicity/AI laws).
